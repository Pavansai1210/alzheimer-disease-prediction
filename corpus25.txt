Alzheimer's Disease Overview
Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that primarily affects memory, thinking, and behavior. It is the most common cause of dementia among older adults. The disease characterized by the accumulation of amyloid plaques and tau tangles in the brain, leading to the death of nerve cells and loss of brain tissue over time.

Symptoms of Alzheimer's Disease
Early Stage (Mild):
- Memory loss of recent events or conversations
- Difficulty performing familiar tasks
- Problems with language and finding the right words
- Disorientation in space and time
- Poor judgment and decision-making
- Changes in mood or personality

Middle Stage (Moderate):
- Increased memory loss and confusion
- Problems recognizing family and friends
- Difficulty with multi-step tasks like dressing
- Impulsive behavior or wandering
- Hallucinations or paranoia in some cases

Late Stage (Severe):
- Loss of ability to communicate
- Significant weight loss and difficulty swallowing
- Loss of bowel and bladder control
- Total dependence on caregivers for daily activities

Medicines for Alzheimer's
While there is no cure, several medications are used to manage symptoms:
- Donepezil (Aricept): Used for all stages of Alzheimer's.
- Rivastigmine (Exelon): Used for mild to moderate dementia.
- Galantamine (Razadyne): Used for mild to moderate symptoms.
- Memantine (Namenda): Used for moderate to severe symptoms.
- Lecanemab (Leqembi): A newer therapy that targets amyloid plaques to slow progression in early stages.
- Donanemab (Kisunla): Another amyloid-targeting therapy for early Alzheimer's.

Precautions and Prevention
Lifestyle Measures:
- Regular Physical Exercise: 30 minutes of aerobic exercise most days.
- Healthy Diet: Following a Mediterranean or MIND diet high in fruits and vegetables.
- Mental Stimulation: Challenging the brain with new activities, reading, or puzzles.
- Social Engagement: Staying active in the community and with loved ones.
- Cardiovascular Health: Controlling blood pressure and cholesterol levels.

Home Safety for Patients:
- Remove tripping hazards like rugs and extension cords.
- Install grab bars in bathrooms.
- Ensure adequate lighting throughout the home.
- Use auto-shutoff appliances in the kitchen.
- Secure dangerous items like cleaning supplies and tools.
