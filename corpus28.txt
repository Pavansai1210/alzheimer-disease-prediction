Bronchitis Overview
Bronchitis is an inflammation of the lining of your bronchial tubes, which carry air to and from your lungs. People who have bronchitis often cough up thickened mucus, which can be discolored. Bronchitis may be either acute or chronic. Acute bronchitis is very common and usually results from a cold or other respiratory infection. Chronic bronchitis, a more serious condition, is a constant irritation or inflammation of the lining of the bronchial tubes, often due to smoking.

Symptoms of Bronchitis
- Persistent cough with mucus
- Fatigue and shortness of breath
- Slight fever and chills
- Chest discomfort
- Wheezing

Medicines for Bronchitis
- Cough suppressants
- Bronchodilators (Inhalers)
- Anti-inflammatory drugs
- Antibiotics (only if bacterial)

Precautions and Management
- Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke
- Use a humidifier to ease breathing
- Drink plenty of fluids to thin mucus
- Get plenty of rest
- Wear a mask in polluted environments
