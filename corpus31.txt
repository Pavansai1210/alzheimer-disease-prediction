Cholera Overview
Cholera is an acute diarrheal illness caused by infection of the intestine with the bacterium Vibrio cholerae. People can get sick when they swallow food or water contaminated with cholera bacteria. The infection is often mild or without symptoms, but can sometimes be severe and life-threatening. About 1 in 10 people with cholera will experience severe symptoms, which can lead to rapid dehydration and shock.

Symptoms of Cholera
- Severe watery diarrhea (rice-water stools)
- Vomiting and nausea
- Rapid heart rate
- Loss of skin elasticity
- Low blood pressure
- Muscle cramps due to salt loss

Medicines for Cholera
- Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)
- Intravenous fluids (severe cases)
- Antibiotics (Doxycycline, Azithromycin)
- Zinc supplements (especially for children)

Precautions and Management
- Drink only safe water (boiled, treated or bottled)
- Wash hands frequently with soap and safe water
- Use toilets for waste disposal
- Ensure food is cooked thoroughly and served hot
- Avoid raw or undercooked seafood
