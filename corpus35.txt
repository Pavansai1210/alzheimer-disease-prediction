Gastritis Overview
Gastritis is a general term for a group of conditions with one thing in common: inflammation of the lining of the stomach. The inflammation of gastritis is most often the result of infection with the same bacterium that causes most stomach ulcers or the regular use of certain pain relievers. Drinking too much alcohol also can contribute to gastritis. Gastritis may occur suddenly (acute) or develop slowly over time (chronic).

Symptoms of Gastritis
- Gnawing or burning ache in upper abdomen
- Nausea and vomiting
- Feeling of fullness in upper abdomen after eating
- Loss of appetite
- Indigestion

Medicines for Gastritis
- Antibiotics (if H. pylori is present)
- Proton pump inhibitors (Omeprazole, Lansoprazole)
- H2 blockers (Famotidine)
- Antacids (for quick relief)

Precautions and Management
- Eat smaller, more frequent meals
- Avoid irritating foods (spicy, acidic, fried)
- Limit or avoid alcohol consumption
- Manage stress
- Be cautious with NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Aspirin)
