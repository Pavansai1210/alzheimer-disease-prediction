Measles Overview
Measles is a highly contagious respiratory infection caused by a virus. It causes a total body skin rash and flu-like symptoms. Measles is so contagious that if one person has it, up to 90% of the people close to that person who are not immune will also become infected. Measles can be serious and even fatal for small children. While death rates have been falling worldwide as more children receive the measles vaccine, the disease still kills more than 100,000 people a year.

Symptoms of Measles
- High fever
- Cough and runny nose
- Red, watery eyes (conjunctivitis)
- Tiny white spots inside the mouth (Koplik spots)
- A skin rash made up of large, flat blotches

Medicines for Measles
- Fever reducers (Acetaminophen, Ibuprofen)
- Vitamin A (especially for children)
- Antibiotics (only for secondary infections like pneumonia)
- Post-exposure vaccination (within 72 hours)

Precautions and Management
- Get vaccinated (MMR vaccine)
- Isolate the infected person to prevent spreading
- Drink plenty of fluids
- Rest to allow the immune system to recover
- Use a humidifier to soothe a cough or sore throat
