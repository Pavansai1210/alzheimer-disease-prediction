Scabies Overview
Scabies is an itchy skin rash caused by a tiny burrowing mite called Sarcoptes scabiei. The intense itching is caused by the body's allergic reaction to the mites, their eggs and their waste. Scabies is contagious and can spread quickly through close physical contact in a family, child care group, school, nursing home or prison. Because scabies is so contagious, doctors often recommend treatment for entire families.

Symptoms of Scabies
- Intense itching, usually worse at night
- Thin, wavy tunnels made of tiny blisters or bumps
- Rash in skin folds (between fingers, armpits, genitals)
- Small sores from scratching

Medicines for Scabies
- Permethrin cream (Elimite)
- Crotamiton cream
- Ivermectin (oral medication for severe cases)
- Sulfur ointment
- Antihistamines for itching

Precautions and Management
- Wash all clothes and bedding in hot water
- Vacuum all rooms in the home
- Avoid direct skin-to-skin contact with infected persons
- Treat all family members simultaneously
- Cut fingernails to prevent skin damage from scratching
