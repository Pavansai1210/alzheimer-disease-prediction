Scurvy Overview
Scurvy is a disease caused by a severe deficiency of vitamin C (ascorbic acid). It was common among sailors in the past when fresh fruits and vegetables were hard to get on long voyages. Today, scurvy is rare in developed countries but can still affect people with very restricted diets. Vitamin C is essential for making collagen, which the body uses to build and repair tissues.

Symptoms of Scurvy
- Fatigue and weakness
- Sore arms and legs
- Bleeding or swollen gums
- Bruising easily
- Small red or purple spots on the skin
- Poor wound healing
- Anemia

Medicines for Scurvy
- Vitamin C supplements (high dose initially)
- Ascorbic acid tablets
- Multivitamins

Precautions and Management
- Eat plenty of fresh fruits and vegetables
- Include citrus fruits (oranges, lemons) in your diet
- Eat tomatoes, broccoli, peppers and strawberries
- Avoid overcooking vegetables (can destroy vitamin C)
- Manage underlying conditions that affect nutrient absorption
