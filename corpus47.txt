Typhoid Fever Overview
Typhoid fever is a life-threatening infection caused by the bacterium Salmonella Typhi. It is usually spread through contaminated food or water. Once the bacteria are eaten or drunk, they multiply and spread into the bloodstream. In countries where sanitation is poor, typhoid fever is more common. Most people recover with antibiotic treatment, but some may develop complications.

Symptoms of Typhoid Fever
- High fever that increases daily
- Headache and fatigue
- Muscle aches
- Sweating
- Dry cough
- Loss of appetite and weight loss
- Stomach pain and diarrhea or constipation

Medicines for Typhoid Fever
- Antibiotics (Ciprofloxacin, Ceftriaxone)
- Azithromycin
- Corticosteroids (for severe cases)
- Fluids (to prevent dehydration)

Precautions and Management
- Get vaccinated before traveling to high-risk areas
- Drink only boiled or bottled water
- Wash hands frequently with soap
- Avoid raw fruits and vegetables that can't be peeled
- Eat food that is cooked and served hot
- Complete the full course of antibiotics
