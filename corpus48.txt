Ulcer Overview
A peptic ulcer is a sore on the lining of your stomach, small intestine or esophagus. Peptic ulcers are most commonly caused by the bacterium H. pylori and long-term use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen and naproxen sodium. Stress and spicy foods do not cause peptic ulcers; however, they can make your symptoms worse.

Symptoms of Ulcer
- Burning stomach pain
- Feeling of fullness, bloating or belching
- Intolerance to fatty foods
- Heartburn
- Nausea

Medicines for Ulcer
- Antibiotics (to kill H. pylori)
- Proton pump inhibitors (Omeprazole, Lansoprazole)
- H2 blockers (Famotidine)
- Antacids (to neutralize stomach acid)
- Cytoprotective agents (Sucralfate)

Precautions and Management
- Limit or avoid alcohol consumption
- Quit smoking (slows healing)
- Be cautious with NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Aspirin)
- Manage stress effectively
- Eat small, regular meals
- Identify and avoid foods that trigger pain
